# What a system changes when it changes itself

**One line:** A conceptual paper that sorts self-modification by what is changed: actions only, low-level rules, control rules, or the criterion used to judge changes. It argues current AI can be changed from outside at low levels without a matching self-representation.

**Why it matters to us:** Its top level, "revising the evaluation criterion", is what we call taste becoming self-originated. It gives section 2 a vocabulary for how far up the ladder a self-edit reaches.

Source: [arXiv 2603.27611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27611) (v2, 3 Sep 2026). Abstract only; full text not read. Not empirical.

## Claim
Self-modification should be described by two axes: how deep the changed target is, and whether the change is blind or reflexive (informed by a self-representation) ([abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27611)).

## Mechanism
- A minimal model with three parts: functional rules, modification mechanisms, evaluation criteria.
- Four regimes: action without change, change of low-level rules, change of control rules, revision of the evaluation criterion.
- "Crossed-opacities" hypothesis: humans describe themselves well at abstract levels and poorly at implementation levels; current AI may be the reverse, open to outside change at low levels with no inner access.

## Limits / what they didn't show
- No experiments. The proposed empirical tests are for future work.

## Relevance to taste-lab
**Relevance 2. Section 2, and a frame for section 5.**
- Places [[A model rewrites its own weights (Fan 2026)]] at "change of low-level rules, reflexive": it reads its own states but keeps an outside criterion.
- Our target is the fourth regime: the model revises its own evaluation criterion. The paper names the difficulty there (how to evaluate a change to the evaluator), which is our collapse problem.
