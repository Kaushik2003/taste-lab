# Metacognition and Confidence Dynamics in Advice Taking from Generative AI

**One line:** Asking an AI for advice is not neutral: merely receiving advice causally raises confidence both in the AI and in oneself, whether or not the advice helped.

**Why it matters to us:** Confidence inflation from mere exposure is a confound for every human-AI study that uses self-reported trust, and a mechanism behind over-reliance. For the review's human side: the user's monitor is as hackable as the model's.

Source: [arXiv 2510.26508](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26508) (30 October 2025). Abstract-level note.

## Claim
Prospective confidence (in self and in GenAI) drives advice seeking; advice exposure in turn causally boosts retrospective confidence in both the AI and the self ([abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26508)).

## Mechanism
- Study 1 (N=200): participants plan events, may request GenAI advice. Study 2 (N=300): advice assigned randomly; rely or ignore.

## Evidence
- Advice requests correlate with higher prospective confidence in GenAI and lower confidence in self ([abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26508)).
- Random assignment shows advice exposure increases confidence in GenAI and in self - a causal effect of exposure, not of quality.

## Limits / what they didn't show
- Event-planning text tasks; not correctness-checkable domains.
- Confidence measured by self-report only.
- No measure of whether the advice was actually good.

## Relevance to taste-lab
**Relevance 2. Section 3.**
- The human half of the calibration-gap story with Steyvers: both sides of the interaction mis-track confidence.
- A design warning for any human-in-the-loop taste evaluation: asking a human to rate model outputs after seeing model reasoning is contaminated.

## Steal this
- The prospective/retrospective confidence split as the minimal instrumentation for any human-AI study.
