# AI Makes You Smarter but None the Wiser

**One line:** Using an LLM raises task performance but breaks self-assessment: users cannot tell how well they did, the usual Dunning-Kruger gap between good and poor performers flattens, and higher AI literacy predicts *worse* self-assessment.

**Why it matters to us:** The human-monitor degradation result: outsourcing cognition to the model quietly downgrades the user's own monitoring. For the review this is the "who watches the watcher" problem at the societal scale, and it sets up the argument that model-side monitors must be explicit because the human fallback is eroding.

Source: [Computers in Human Behavior](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2025.108779) (2025). Abstract via search snippets; full text paywalled.

## Claim
LLM use improves performance while degrading metacognitive accuracy: people misjudge how well they performed when using AI, and the Dunning-Kruger pattern levels out across ability levels ([paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2025.108779)).

## Mechanism
- Behavioral study comparing performance and self-assessment with and without LLM assistance (details paywalled).

## Evidence
- Users cannot accurately assess their performance when using AI ([highlights](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2025.108779)).
- LLM usage levels out the Dunning-Kruger effect.
- Higher AI literacy and higher confidence both correlate with lower self-assessment accuracy.

## Limits / what they didn't show
- Highlights-level access only; sample sizes and task types unverified here.
- Levelling of Dunning-Kruger could mean everyone's self-assessment converges to mediocre, not that experts got worse.

## Relevance to taste-lab
**Relevance 2. Section 6 (risks/limits).**
- The third piece of the human-side failure triad: exposure inflates confidence (Colombatto), fluent non-signals bypass vigilance (Maynard), and the user's own monitoring degrades with use (this paper).
- Motivates explicit, inspectable model-side monitoring: the human backstop cannot be assumed.

## Steal this
- "Smarter but none the wiser" as the one-line summary of the human-side risk, with the AI-literacy reversal as the surprising detail.
