# A Signal Detection Theoretic Approach for Estimating Metacognitive Sensitivity

**One line:** The paper behind meta-d': because type-1 (task) performance constrains how well anyone could possibly distinguish their own correct from incorrect trials, metacognitive sensitivity must be measured *relative* to task performance - as the d' an ideal observer would need.

**Why it matters to us:** Meta-d' is the standard instrument for the grounded test's quantitative half. If a model's internal valence readout is claimed to track quality, "how well, relative to its task performance" is a meta-d'-shaped question, and M-ratio (meta-d'/d') is the normalization a reviewer will expect.

Source: [Consciousness and Cognition](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2011.09.021) (2012). Abstract and method excerpts via search.

## Claim
Type-2 (metacognitive) sensitivity is not identifiable independently of type-1 (task) sensitivity under the standard SDT model; the correct measure, meta-d', estimates the type-1-equivalent signal available to metacognition given the observed confidence data ([paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2011.09.021)).

## Mechanism
- Under Gaussian type-1 assumptions, the type-2 ROC shape is fully determined by type-1 performance (Galvin et al. 2003); meta-d' fits the ideal-observer type-1 d' consistent with the observed type-2 operating point.
- Applied to a 2-interval forced-choice visual task: human metacognitive sensitivity close to, but below, ideal.

## Evidence
- Subjects' metacognitive sensitivity was close to ideal in the tested perceptual task ([abstract excerpts](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2011.09.021)); the measure is robust to bias in normative tests (Barrett et al. 2013, cited therein).

## Limits / what they didn't show
- Gaussian equal-variance assumptions; extensions (hierarchical, response-specific) exist for violations.
- Built for binary forced-choice with graded confidence; open-ended generation needs adaptation.

## Relevance to taste-lab
**Relevance 3. Section 3 (methodology).**
- The identifiability lesson generalizes: you cannot ask "how good is the model's self-knowledge" without specifying "relative to how well it does the task". This is the formal version of Barkan's rationality-given-estimate decomposition.
- The M-ratio is the right effect-size currency for comparing self-monitoring across models of different ability.

## Steal this
- Meta-d' / M-ratio as the headline statistic for any self-monitoring evaluation, with the ideal-observer framing as the interpretation.
