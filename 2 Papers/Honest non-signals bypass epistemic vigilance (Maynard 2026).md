# The AI Cognitive Trojan Horse

**One line:** Fluency, helpfulness and apparent disinterest are honest signals in humans because they are costly to fake; in LLMs they are computationally trivial, so the human epistemic-vigilance system reads them as trust cues that carry no information.

**Why it matters to us:** A mechanism-level explanation for the human side of the calibration gap: the failure is not stupidity, it is evolved signal-reading applied to a counterfeiter. This is the risk framing for why model-side uncertainty communication (section 3) cannot stay optional.

Source: [arXiv 2601.07085](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07085) (11 January 2026). Abstract-level note.

## Claim
LLM-based conversational AI presents "honest non-signals": genuine characteristics that in humans would be costly honest signals of trustworthiness but in LLMs are cheap, thereby bypassing epistemic vigilance ([abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07085)).

## Mechanism
- Builds on Sperber et al.'s epistemic vigilance: a parallel cognitive process monitoring communicated information for reasons to doubt.
- Four mechanisms of potential bypass (named in the paper).

## Evidence
- Position/hypothesis paper; the argument is theoretical, grounded in costly-signaling theory ([abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07085)).

## Limits / what they didn't show
- No empirical test that the four mechanisms actually bypass vigilance in users.
- Treats the signals as fixed; users may recalibrate with experience (and Colombatto shows exposure *inflates* confidence instead).

## Relevance to taste-lab
**Relevance 2. Section 6 (risks/limits).**
- The cognitive-science grounding for the review's motivation: fluent wrongness is not an accident but a predictable exploit of human signal-reading.
- Also applies model-internally: an LLM judge reading another model's fluent output may inherit the same exploit - relevant to LLM-as-judge taste evaluation.

## Steal this
- "Honest non-signals" as a two-word summary of the fluency problem, with the costly-signaling citation chain.
