# Imagining and Building Wise Machines

**One line:** A TiCS position paper by a heavyweight author list (Bengio, Mitchell, Rahwan, Scholkopf, Chater, Gerstenberg and others): AI has become smart without becoming wise, and the missing ingredient is metacognition - intellectual humility, perspective-taking, context adaptability.

**Why it matters to us:** The highest-authority statement yet that metacognition is the bottleneck for the next generation of AI, published in the field's main opinion venue. This is cover for the review's core claim at the level reviewers respect, and its benchmarking/training/architecture agenda overlaps our research questions.

Source: [Trends in Cognitive Sciences](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2026.01.002) (2025). Abstract and highlights via search snippets; full text paywalled.

## Claim
Human wisdom handles intractable problems through object-level strategies (managing problems) plus metacognitive strategies (managing the object-level strategies); AI particularly struggles with the metacognitive kind, and wiser AI would be more robust, explainable, cooperative and safer ([paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2026.01.002)).

## Mechanism
- Position paper: imports the psychology of wisdom (intellectual humility, perspective-taking, context adaptability) as the spec for AI metacognition.
- Proposes approaches for benchmarking wisdom, training wise reasoning strategies, and adapting AI architectures for metacognition.

## Evidence
- Opinion article; the argument is a synthesis of the wisdom literature plus analysis of current AI failure modes ([abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2026.01.002)).

## Limits / what they didn't show
- A vision, not a system: no implemented wise machine, no benchmark delivered yet.
- Wisdom is broader than monitoring+control; the mapping from wisdom psychology to measurable model properties is asserted, not operationalized.
