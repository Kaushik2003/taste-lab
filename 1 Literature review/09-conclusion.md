# 9. Conclusion

This review followed monitor, evaluate, control and persist through the literature on metacognition in reasoning models. These stages have overlapping prior art, not merely isolated pieces: confidence can influence abstention, hidden-state competence predictions can guide learning in MAGELLAN, and Fan can choose a direct self-edit while graded answers govern retention. Quality evaluation also exists beyond correctness: Kirin v3 reads human preference from activations, Gao steers readability-related representations, and Yuan trains on the same model's verbal quality judgments. None of these results alone establishes the proposed activation-level readability loop or self-originated taste. Collapse and reward exploitation remain risks, not universal impossibility results.

**Bounded contribution.** We propose to test whether a causally validated activation-level evaluation readout can guide output selection and subsequent self-training for readability among programs matched on observed test outcomes, with held-out audits of quality and drift. We have not identified evidence for that complete combination in the studies reviewed; this is a scoped research proposal rather than proof that no prior work exists. Section 8's closest-prior-art table states the distinctions. A grounded readout needs causal testing beyond decodability, and self-originated preference needs evidence beyond human agreement or own-output bias.

Code readability among programs matched on observed test outcomes offers a constrained testbed. Tests do not remove all functional, efficiency or robustness differences, so passing must not be equated with complete correctness equivalence. The research sequence follows from the evidence. First, the gating experiment: does a valence readout track blind human readability rankings among passing solutions, beating layer-0 probes, readability directions and correctness-value baselines, after length, perplexity and comment density are partialled out ([[Q1 Does the model sense code quality]], exp01)? If it does, the second step is inference-time control, with the evidence favoring best-of-n selection by the readout over direct steering, audited against a hidden readability anchor for judge hacking ([[Q2 Can the model steer its own code]]). Only then does self-training on the signal become worth testing, behind tests, a novelty term, collapse metrics and a matched random-reward control ([[Q3 Can self-learning avoid collapse]]). If the gating experiment fails - the readout does not track readability beyond cheap confounds - every downstream thread loses its evaluator, and the contribution can be a negative result about this readout and testbed, with the stated controls.

Beyond the loop sits the architecture question this review opened with. The field has the legs of a self-developing metacognition - persistent latent self-state, self-modeling world models, learned selection and update machinery - and a survey-level admission that motivation and metacognition are its under-researched functions ([[World models miss motivation and metacognition (Rupprecht 2026)]]). What joins them is an internal evaluation signal that decides what is worth keeping. If the loop above works even in the narrow readability testbed, it is that signal, and the architecture is the paper after this one.
