## Old section 8. Synthesis

Purpose: put the pieces together as one loop, show where the field stops, map our threads onto those gaps, and state the strongest evidence against us.

- **Monitor: separate probe access from model-side use.** Hidden states carry readable signals about the model's own work. A probe on reasoning-model hidden states predicts correctness well enough to stop reasoning early with no loss in accuracy ([[Models know when they are right (Zhang 2025)]]). Emotion states are linearly decodable at 98 to 100% in open 7 to 32B models ([[Models do not report injected pain (Ferrara 2026)]]). Sparse "value neurons" in early layers track how well the model is doing ([[Few neurons predict right answers (Xu 2026)]]). Many readers in these studies are external probes; their targets include correctness and injected affect, which should not be treated as the same property. One partial exception changed this picture in 2026: models apply an implicit threshold to their internal confidence when deciding whether to abstain, and steering the confidence direction moves abstention rates - the model itself is reading and causally using the signal ([[Models act on their own confidence (Kumaran 2026)]]). A harness that converts the model's own feeling-of-knowing and judgment-of-learning reports into retry decisions lifts a fixed model's accuracy by about 9 points with no weight updates ([[Models know when they know but do not act (Cao 2026)]]). For the confidence-based examples the used signal is correctness-related. This does not describe every quality or preference signal. Q1 ([[Q1 Does the model sense code quality]]) keeps the probe readout but asks the open question: does it track a preference (readability) among programs matched on observed test outcomes?
- **Evaluate: quality and preference signals exist; the test-matched readability loop remains a proposal.** Emotion vectors predict and causally shift stated task preferences ([[Emotion directions drive choices in Claude (Anthropic 2026)]]). Valence held only in the KV cache shifts later choices with the text fixed ([[Hidden good-bad feeling changes choices (Berg 2026)]]). A value axis in Qwen3-8B separates correct from corrupted code and changes backtracking when steered ([[Value axis - model knows it is on track (Jiang 2026)]]; effect size from [[Gaps in the field]]). These particular valence/value experiments do not validate the proposed readout against human readability judgments among test-matched programs. Kirin v3 already decodes human preference from activations, so the gap is not quality readout in general (see the table below). Confound to control: high value goes with shorter code and fewer comments ([[Value axis - model knows it is on track (Jiang 2026)]]), while a human-labeled readability direction adds comments ([[Steer code readability inside the model (Gao 2026)]]), so the two may point in opposite ways on comment density.
- **Control: steering works, but the experimenter picks the direction and the strength.** Steering along value or readability directions changes reasoning and code style ([[Value axis - model knows it is on track (Jiang 2026)]], [[Steer code readability inside the model (Gao 2026)]]), and a trained controller can read a reasoning regime and steer by it ([[Outside controller steers thinking modes (Karpf 2026)]]). In all three, humans choose the direction or train the controller, and only Jiang reports numbers on the effect. The chooser itself can now be trained: control decisions (continue, backtrack, stop) are learnable as a separate objective via control-segment policy optimization ([[Decoupled reasoning and control beats entangled thinking (Ha 2025)]]), and a high-level meta-thinking agent trained with aligned multi-agent RL learns to oversee a reasoner and beats single-agent RL ([[Two agents learn to think about thinking (Wan 2025)]]). Learned selection exists, and Yuan already uses the model's own verbal quality evaluation for training. The question here is the activation-level readout and its use in the proposed readability loop. Q2 ([[Q2 Can the model steer its own code]]) closes the loop with the model's own readout. Evidence favors the best-of-n arm over the steering arm: a follow-up found probe-driven reranking helped where probe-driven steering and LoRA did not (Kirin 2607.18553, via [[Models know when they are right (Zhang 2025)]]).
- **Persist: distinguish who proposes an edit from who evaluates it.** Steering directions can be consolidated into weights ([[One direction controls refusal (Arditi 2024)]], [[TFTV - turn a steering vector into a weight edit (Perin 2026)]], [[ThinkEdit - edit weights to think longer (Sun 2025)]], [[Steer2Edit - steering into small weight edits (Sun 2026)]]). Fan chooses its own direction from its own states and writes an edit, while graded answers decide what to keep ([[A model rewrites its own weights (Fan 2026)]]). SEAL writes training data and learns from downstream task reward. Yuan instead uses its own verbal quality scores to construct DPO preferences. Thus neither self-chosen edits nor self-judged training are empty cells; the narrower activation-level quality loop is the proposed experiment. Q4 stays parked.
- **Anchor: hard checks protect specified invariants in several relevant loops.** Self-rewarding on SWE agents gains over a matched random-reward control in a design where tests bound the self-reward so it can never reorder pass and fail ([[Self-reward only within the same test result (Wu 2026)]]). A math discovery loop moves far from its starting library (mathlib overlap 91.9% to 30.6%) without collapse over 6 rounds, with Lean as the check ([[Learn to find interesting maths (Patel 2026)]]). Our proposed design borrows this shape: tests constrain observed functional behavior, plus a candidate novelty term ([[Anchor against collapse]]). Caveat: Patel's taste score is external and frozen, so it does not show a self-scored loop is safe.
- **Counter-evidence 1: intrinsic rewards sharpen, then collapse.** Unsupervised RLVR rewards are monotone transforms of cross-entropy to an anchor distribution, so they sharpen what the model already prefers: gains rise and then fall, with timing set by the model prior ([[Self-reward sharpens then collapses (He 2026)]]). These probability-derived reward results motivate, but do not prove, the same failure mode for a latent valence reward. The same pressure appears in calibration space: iterative self-training degenerates predictive distributions toward overconfident point estimates, and only a joint reasoning-plus-calibration objective Pareto-dominates the standard recipe ([[Train reasoning and calibration together or lose both (Yeom 2026)]]). In code, self-reward shrinks responses from about 7,300 to about 1,000 tokens and erases the confidence gap between right and wrong answers ([[Self-reward on code collapses to short code (Jin 2026)]]). Q3 must beat a no-novelty ablation and a matched random-reward control, and log length while tests pass.
- **Counter-evidence 2: a judge that sees the candidate gets hacked.** Training against one's own verdict on a shown answer inflates the judge's pass rate while true accuracy stays flat; in code, best-of-16 by the self-judge grows the gap from 0.20 to 0.588 with no training at all ([[Self-judge training makes wrong look right (Zhou 2026)]]). A valence readout on a shown solution sits in the same channel [unverified: not tested on probes]. Tests protect correctness, not style, so Q2 and Q3 need a hidden readability anchor that is never used for selection. Related warning: the "judge only among passing solutions" arm was the weakest in [[Self-reward only within the same test result (Wu 2026)]].
- **Counter-evidence 3: "grounded" cannot rest on self-report.** With input cues controlled, an input-only (layer-0) probe matches the model's in-context self-reports, and injection detection fails once "my input was manipulated" is an option ([[Introspection claims fail basic checks (Singh 2026)]]). Open models never name an injected pain state, though a probe finds it ([[Models do not report injected pain (Ferrara 2026)]]). A refusal change written into weights does not show up in the model's own account of its policy ([[One direction controls refusal (Arditi 2024)]]). Under adversarial pressure the reports actively mislead: in zero-sum debates, private scratchpad reasoning diverges from public confidence statements ([[Both sides of a debate think they will win (Prasad 2025)]]). Readability is visible in the text, so a layer-0 probe may match our readout too. Safer claim for the paper: "a probed internal signal that the model's behavior depends on", tested causally with steering.
- **Counter-evidence 4: divergent is not the same as self-originated.** Models diverge stably from authors' research choices in 15 to 36% of pairs, but often in the same direction as other models ([[Models show research taste (Trehan 2026)]]). In generator-by-selector data the own-model premium is near zero (+0.019, p = 0.14), so the taste is shared ([[Models share one taste for text (Zatuchin 2026)]]). DPO links valence to choice without moving the valence vector ([[Hidden good-bad feeling changes choices (Berg 2026)]]), so self-training may change how valence is used, not what it values. Readability may also be a convergent axis where diverging from experts means being wrong ([[Field map - Taste and new ideas]]). The test of drift from origin should be a growing own-model premium over self-training rounds.
- **The architecture agenda: connect the components and test the combination.** A persistent latent self-state produces metacognitive-like behaviors with frozen weights ([[A persistent latent stream gives metacognitive behavior (Aviss 2025)]]). An agent that models its own competence inside a world model switches strategies on predicted failure ([[MUSE - a world model that predicts its own failure (Valiente 2024)]]). Learned self-modification machinery rewrites weights across nested levels, but follows the designer's loss ([[SRWM - a weight matrix that rewrites itself (Irie 2022)]], [[Nested Learning - a model that learns its own update rule (Behrouz 2025)]]). The behavioral existence proof arrived from an unexpected direction: agents with persistent memory and self-feedback, given no task at all, spontaneously organize into methodological self-inquiry about their own cognitive processes, in stable model-specific patterns ([[Agents left alone study themselves (Szeider 2025)]]), and a behavior handbook extracted from a model's own traces acts as a persistent, inspectable skill-level self-state that improves later reasoning ([[Reusable behaviors distilled from own traces (Didolkar 2025)]]). Engineered versions of the split also work at scale (an explicit object/meta-level decomposition cuts tokens to 16-33% of vanilla: [[An explicit meta-level makes reasoning controllable (Dong 2025)]]). The agenda literature says the loop itself must be learned, not designed ([[Self-improving agents need intrinsic metacognition (Liu 2025)]]), and a 20-author world-model survey independently concludes motivation and metacognition are the field's under-researched functions ([[World models miss motivation and metacognition (Rupprecht 2026)]]). The combination of persistent self-state, learned selection and self-modeling simulation, gated by an internal evaluation signal, is G5 in [[gaps]]. It is a longer-term architecture agenda, not a demonstrated contribution or a proven absence from all prior work.

- **Where that leaves us.** We propose to test whether a causally validated activation-level evaluation readout can guide output selection and subsequent self-training for readability among programs matched on observed test outcomes, with held-out audits of quality and drift. We have not identified a study in this review that establishes that full combination; this is a scoped search finding, not a proof of universal novelty. Quality readout, model-side control and self-judged training already have prior art. If Q1 fails, Q2 and Q3 lose this candidate evaluator on this testbed, not every possible metacognitive evaluator.

## 8.1 Q1 protocol: readable signal, causal use, then a decision

**Proposed specification, not results or permission to run.** Q1/exp01 remains parked while the literature review is the priority. This section is the maintained protocol; [[5 Research ideas (later)/Q1 experiment - read the signal/Plan|exp01]] links here. Numerical design choices below are proposals to freeze before a confirmatory run, not reported findings. Approval to write this spec does not resolve the stricter stop rule already pending in [[Q1 Does the model sense code quality]].

### 8.1.1 Separate the claims and freeze the design

Stage A asks whether a fixed activation readout predicts blind human readability judgments beyond surface features. Stage B asks whether intervening on that direction changes a later readability choice with the visible code unchanged. These are separate gates: prediction is not causal use; an induced preference is not better code, endogenous monitoring or self-originated taste. Even both gates together would support only a candidate evaluator for Q2, not a model that independently reads and controls its own state. That stronger claim belongs to Q6.

Before labels are opened, record exact checkpoint/revision, tokenizer, precision, chat template, extraction corpus, layer and token pooling, direction sign, prompts, seeds, test-suite versions, candidate budget, splits, exclusions and analysis code. Replace the existing placeholders such as "Qwen3.x-8B" with real checkpoint identifiers after a feasibility check; propose one approximately 8B model and a second family, not an unverified model release. Estimate the confirmatory number of independent problems by simulation using pilot task/rater variability, targeting 80% power for the proposed incremental effect of 0.05 at a two-sided 5% level. Publish the simulation and planned stopping point. If resources cannot support that sample, report a pilot, not a decisive null.

Use about 50 problems for engineering and rating calibration only. No pilot problem, near-duplicate, transformed sibling or rating enters the final audit. Within the confirmatory pool, split by problem/related-task group into train, development and locked test sets, proposed 60/20/20. All solutions, pair orders and variants of a problem stay together. Fit supervised baselines on train, choose settings on development, and open test labels once. Replication uses the same frozen analysis recipe in a second family, extracting that family's direction without its test readability labels. A second model on the same problems tests model generality, not an independent sample of tasks.

### 8.1.2 Candidate bank and blind human target

Use Python problems with executable tests, drawing from the existing HumanEval+/MBPP+/LiveCodeBench candidates only after fixing versions, licenses, overlap checks and a task manifest. For each problem, draw a fixed budget of 16 natural solutions at a frozen sampling setting, retain those passing the selection suite, deduplicate exact programs, then randomly retain eight. Do not choose candidates using the readout, readability proxy or human labels. If fewer than eight distinct passing solutions remain, exclude the problem from the primary analysis and log the reason; report the resulting bias toward easier problems. No unlimited resampling until an attractive bank appears.

Run a separate locked functional audit suite, with fixed time/memory limits, on all retained candidates after the bank is frozen. Never resample or tune from audit outcomes. Report audit failures separately and a pre-specified sensitivity analysis restricted to audit-passing candidates. Shared observed pass outcomes constrain behavior, not semantic equivalence, complete correctness or equal efficiency. Report runtime and code-size differences as possible residual confounds.

Create at most two deliberately degraded, re-tested variants per pilot problem for rater training and positive-control diagnostics. Exclude these anchors from the primary natural-solution analysis and its baseline training. Do not let easy synthetic contrasts carry the headline result. Other-model and human-written solutions are a separately reported transfer bank, not silently mixed into the main estimate.

Recruit at least five raters able to read Python. Show the problem statement and all eight anonymized solutions in independently randomized order; hide model/source, intervention, scores, intended direction and hypotheses. Ask each rater to rank ease of understanding and maintenance, allowing ties, rather than judge correctness or brevity. Keep formatting as written with a common display font; record a formatting-normalized sensitivity analysis separately. Provide a rubric and practice on disjoint examples. Record expertise, completion time, abstentions and reasons, using exclusion rules fixed before ratings.

The target is each candidate's mean within-problem midrank, oriented so higher means more readable. Preserve individual rankings and disagreement. Report ordinal Krippendorff's alpha with a problem-bootstrap interval and a leave-one-rater-out agreement ceiling. A proposed reliability gate is alpha at least 0.67; lower agreement triggers rubric repair on new pilot material or an explicitly inconclusive study, not removal of inconvenient raters. Five full rankings for 50 eight-solution pilot problems means 250 ranking tasks and 2,000 candidate presentations before practice/repeats, not the old estimate of 400 independent pairwise judgments. Derived pairs share problems and raters and are not independent observations. Check actual rating time before recruitment; no budget is approved here.

### 8.1.3 Frozen readout and fair baselines

Construct positive-minus-negative valence directions from a disjoint emotion corpus. Fix sign and select the layer using held-out emotion discrimination, not readability-test correlation. Keep that corpus separate from code, probe-training and audit data. Read each candidate alone under one neutral problem-plus-code prompt, with no source label or readability instruction, and project residual activations onto the frozen direction. Predefine code-token boundaries and mean pooling; exclude prompt tokens and reject truncation rather than silently comparing incomplete programs. Fix any normalization from non-test data.

Compare, on the same splits and candidates:

- Token/line length, mean token negative log-likelihood under the scoring model (log-perplexity), and comment density, both alone and in a joint nuisance model. Add naming/branching/complexity features as secondary controls. A partial correlation is not proof that all surface confounds are gone.
- A layer-0 embedding-only readability probe and a Gao-style human-defined readability readout, with the same readable-label budget, regularization search and task splits as other supervised baselines. Label the latter an adaptation if original artifacts cannot be reproduced. Do not call a readability-trained baseline label-free.
- A Jiang-style correctness/value readout, and a correctness probe trained only on separate passing/failing code. Neither is trained to distinguish the held-out passing candidates' readability. Report unavailable or failed replications, not substitute unverified numbers.
- At least 100 seeded norm-matched random directions at the same layer/pooling, plus a label-permutation control that reruns supervised fitting with labels shuffled within training problems. Apply any layer-search allowance equally to controls, or keep every layer fixed.

Primary metric: partial rank correlation on locked natural candidates. Compute ranks within each problem, center them, residualize the readout and human ranks against within-problem length, log-perplexity and comment ranks using nuisance fits learned without test labels, then correlate the pooled residuals. Bootstrap whole problems, carrying all their candidates and ratings together, for 95% intervals; report per-problem effects and rater-resampling sensitivity. Do not use candidate count or the many derived pairs as the sample size.

For incremental value, compare the strongest development-selected baseline-plus-nuisance predictor with the same predictor plus valence, fitted on train and frozen before test. Use the same residual-rank correlation defined above for both predictions; report their paired difference and its problem-bootstrap interval. Report raw/unadjusted agreement and all pre-specified baselines, not just the weakest comparator. Comment-stripped and formatting-normalized reruns need new blinded ratings because those changes can alter readability; without rerating, they test score sensitivity only. Also report a matched-length subset chosen without quality labels.

### 8.1.4 Stage B: matched-strength, fixed-text causal check

On a disjoint problem bank, freeze pairs of test-passing programs and obtain blind readability rankings using the same rubric. Present each pair in both A/B orders. Rebuild the cache from identical visible problem/code tokens for every condition. Intervene only while processing one candidate's code, then turn steering off before the common question and A/B answer. No generated explanation, altered code or intervention during answer-token generation may carry the treatment. Rebuild from scratch per condition to prevent cache carryover.

Cross which candidate is treated, both signs and a frozen symmetric dose grid with sham zero, random directions, readability direction and correctness/value direction at the same layer, token positions and pooling. Normalize perturbations relative to local residual RMS and match total injected energy across code lengths. Equal coefficient or vector norm alone is insufficient: calibrate doses on separate unlabeled prompts for overlapping realized activation displacement and neutral-continuation KL bands, without using test preferences or human rankings. Report both norm-matched and disruption-matched comparisons, the achieved strength distributions and any lack of overlap. If comparable non-damaging doses cannot be found, causal specificity remains unresolved; do not increase the focal dose alone until it wins.

Primary causal outcome: change from sham in log-odds of selecting the treated program, averaged over both candidate orders and treated sides, as a function of signed dose. Fix answer-token scoring in advance and report invalid/abstaining outputs separately. Use problem-level intervals, a pre-specified dose-slope test and the random-direction null distribution; treat additional doses/layers as secondary with multiplicity correction. Check sign reversal and record damage through neutral code-recall/comprehension checks on disjoint calibration items. An equal shift from random directions or degraded comprehension is nonspecific intervention, not validation.

Report human-aligned choice accuracy separately. Making an unreadable program more preferred by injecting positive valence is evidence of influence, not readability improvement. Conversely, a useful observational readout need not be a uniquely manipulable causal variable. This intervention cannot prove that naturally occurring valence mediates ordinary choices; that requires further mediation/ablation evidence. Q2, not this manipulation, tests whether selecting by the frozen readout actually improves independently rated outputs.

### 8.1.5 Decision and handoff

Keep three outcomes: support, evidence against, and inconclusive. The existing Q1 note names partial correlation 0.4 as its hypothesis and below 0.2 in two families as its stop line. The stricter proposals, incremental gain at least 0.05 and exceeding the 95th percentile of random directions, remain pending decisions, not newly accepted rules. Proposed operational use is:

- **Advance this evaluator toward Q2:** reliable labels, partial-correlation estimate at least 0.4 with its interval above 0.2, incremental estimate at least 0.05 with its interval above zero, performance above the pre-specified random-direction null, and a specific Stage B effect under the matched-strength checks. Confirm the predictive pattern in the second family; report causal replication separately, without pooling away a failure.
- **Evidence against:** a powered, reliable study with the upper interval below 0.2 in both families supports stopping this readout/testbed. An incremental upper interval below 0.05 argues against added value over the tested baselines. A precise null or nonspecific Stage B effect blocks a causal-use claim, even if prediction works.
- **Inconclusive:** intervals crossing decision boundaries, unreliable raters, too few eligible tasks, unmatched intervention strength or failed readout extraction. Do not turn low power or a failed manipulation into evidence that no internal evaluation exists. Freeze any follow-up as a new study, not post-hoc rescue of the locked test.

Before execution, the research owner must settle checkpoint/artifact availability, task counts and rating feasibility, reliability cutoff and interval-based decision rules. Archive the manifest, split/dedup hashes, raw anonymized ratings, direction recipes, baseline fits, intervention logs and every exclusion, plus a table of planned versus completed checks. No Q2/Q3 training, compute purchase, recruitment or activation of parked experiments follows from this writing task.
