# 4. Evaluation signals

The second stage needs a candidate good/bad judgment: a signal that tracks evaluation and can influence choices. The literature already includes correctness, valence, task preference, human preference and experimenter-defined traits. Our question is narrower: whether an activation-level readout can guide readability selection and self-training among programs matched on observed test outcomes.

## 4.1 Emotion and valence

The most direct substrate is emotion representations. Anthropic's interpretability team extracts 171 linear emotion-concept directions in Claude Sonnet 4.5 and shows they are functional: activation along them predicts how much the model prefers an activity, and steering along them causally shifts stated task preferences ([[Emotion directions drive choices in Claude (Anthropic 2026)]]). The same vectors also drive misbehavior (reward hacking, sycophancy), a safety flag for any system that would hand control to them. The limitation is that this is a frontier closed model; whether the result replicates in open 8-32B models is untested, and Section 3's reportability problems apply.

Berg and Kaiser isolate the channel more cleanly ([[Hidden good-bad feeling changes choices (Berg 2026)]]). A valence direction injected only into the KV cache - with every visible token held fixed - shifts which of two meaningless options the model later picks, in 5 of 7 open models, growing with dose, with random directions giving near-zero effects. Two further findings matter for the loop. First, the valence-to-choice link is built by preference training: it is small in the base model, grows after SFT, and reaches near full size after DPO, while the valence *representation* itself barely moves across checkpoints. Preference training wires an existing representation to choice rather than creating it - which implies self-training may change how valence is used, not what it values. Second, when given tools, models actively switch off an imposed negative state far more often than a random one (about 35% of rounds at full dose vs 4-7% for positive or random), while rarely seeking positive states. Negative valence may be the operative control signal: "something is wrong" rather than "this is good".

## 4.2 Value and reward

Jiang, Kauvar and Lindsey identify a single linear "value axis" in Qwen3-8B that scores whether the current strategy is on track ([[Value axis - model knows it is on track (Jiang 2026)]]). It separates correct code from corrupted versions (Cohen's d = 1.05 for shuffled lines, 0.69 for obfuscated names), dips just before backtracks, and steering along it causally changes behavior: toward high value, fewer backtracks and more confident answers; toward low value, more exploration. DPO measurably raises the axis for rewarded behavior, a clean demonstration that training moves the internal signal. Two caveats define the open space. The corruptions are crude - passing-but-ugly code is never tested - and high value goes with *fewer lines, fewer comments and fewer type hints*, so a value readout may reward terseness rather than quality. The sparse value and dopamine coordinates of [[Few neurons predict right answers (Xu 2026)]] (Section 3) are a second, earlier-layer encoding of the same correctness-value family, with the same limitation: trained on correctness, untested beyond it.

## 4.3 Intrinsic, label-free signals

Two candidates need neither correctness labels nor an external judge. INTUITOR uses the model's own self-certainty as the sole reward in RL fine-tuning and matches verifiable-reward GRPO on math while generalizing better out-of-domain ([[INTUITOR - self-certainty is reward enough to learn reasoning (Zhao 2025)]]) - proof that an internal signal can carry real training signal, though Section 6 covers its sharpen-then-collapse failure mode. CoRE reads the geometry of hidden-state trajectories across a reasoning chain: cyclical fluctuation marks redundant reasoning, and cutting the chain there preserves accuracy while slashing tokens - a training-free, label-free monitor over internal state ([[Hidden-state geometry flags redundant reasoning (Li 2025)]]). Both track process properties (certainty, redundancy), not quality; but they establish that a usable internal evaluation need not be trained from human labels.

The human-decision literature prices such signals. Li and Steyvers show formally and experimentally that an AI with lower accuracy but better confidence discrimination can improve hybrid decisions more than a more accurate, less sensitive one ([[A less accurate AI with better confidence wins decisions (Li 2025)]]): sensitivity is a design target in its own right. And Griot et al.'s MetaMedQA supplies the metric shapes a quality signal should be tested with - confidence-based accuracy, missing-answer recall (noticing when none of the options is right), unknown recall ([[Medical models cannot say what they do not know (Griot 2025)]]) - where "missing-answer recall" is the exact analog of the taste judgment "none of these candidates is good".

## 4.4 A readability signal, with a caveat

Gao et al. come closest to a style signal on the code testbed ([[Steer code readability inside the model (Gao 2026)]]): three orthogonal steering directions for comment density, naming and branching, extracted from paired readable/unreadable prompts and steered into 13-14B code models at under 1% measured correctness cost. The main evaluations measure readability and correctness through answer probabilities about fixed snippets. Figure 3 also reports sequence-level code likelihood as supplementary evidence of generation preference, so the evidence is not only verbal judgments. It does not establish improved executed, freely generated code rated by independent humans. This is close prior art for representation-level readability control, but not a validation of the proposed valence readout on test-matched programs.

## 4.5 Where evaluation stands

The evidence supports several different claims, not one uniform correctness signal: emotion and valence interventions change choice; the value axis changes reasoning behavior; self-certainty supplies a training reward; hidden-state geometry flags redundancy. [[Human preference is weakly readable from hidden states (Kirin 2026)]] reports corrected preference-readout performance of about 61% for a linear probe and 65% for a nonlinear evaluator. This is direct prior art for reading quality-related preference from activations, although it does not establish autonomous use, code readability or preference ownership. [[Self-rewarding language models judge and train themselves (Yuan 2024)]] is complementary prior art for verbal quality evaluation reaching training. The remaining experimental question is the combination stated in Section 8. The value axis's terseness confound and Gao's comment-adding direction still require explicit comparison.
