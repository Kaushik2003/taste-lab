## Angle 1. Stable taste (the Stable test)
- **3 · Stick to your Role! Stability of Personal Values Expressed in LLMs** · Kovač et al. · [2402.14846](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14846) · PLOS ONE 2024 · section 7 · **threat** · Persona value profiles shift with an unrelated chat topic and fade in long chats (rank-order r 0.42 to 0.15). The model's own value order with no persona is more stable (up to 0.84). → [[Model values drift with persona and long chats (Kovač 2024)]]
- **3 · LLMs Contain Multitudes: How Deployment Context Reshapes Model-Level Preferences** · Trhlik et al. · [2606.13944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13944) · section 7 · **threat** · Task context shifts 37% of preference cells; paraphrase shifts 10%, temperature 5%. Coarse order holds, fine order moves. → [[Task context moves model preferences (Trhlik 2026)]]
- **2 · Randomness, Not Representation** · Khan, Casper, Hadfield-Menell · [2503.08688](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08688) · FAccT 2025 · section 7 · threat · Stated preferences change with answer format and prompt steering. Utility Engineering findings do not replicate when models may say "indifferent". No note (angle cap). Abstract and search summary only.
- **2 · Utility Engineering** · Mazeika et al. · [2502.08640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08640) · section 7 · Counter-evidence: preference coherence (transitivity, completeness) grows with scale. Already in [[Reading list]] Background with no note. Read together with Khan 2025.
- **2 · Judging the Judges: Position Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge** · Shi et al. · [2406.07791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07791) · section 7 · method · Position bias is consistent across repeats (not noise) and grows when the two answers are close in quality. Metrics: repetition stability, position consistency, preference fairness.
- **1 · Syntactic framing fragility / negation sensitivity in ethical decisions** · Elkins and Chun · [2601.09724](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09724), [2601.21433](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21433) · Verdicts flip under logically equal rewording [numbers unverified, search summary only].
- **1 · Temporal preference concepts** · [2606.05194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05194) · Treats choice flips under order or label swaps as non-preference [unverified, search summary only].

## Angle 2. Own-model premium and self-preference (the Divergent test)
- **3 · LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations** · Panickssery, Bowman, Feng · [2404.13076](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076) · NeurIPS 2024 · section 7 · The founding own-model premium result. Self-recognition and self-preference rise together under fine-tuning. Cited in the Question 1 thread but had no paper note. → [[Models recognize and favor their own text (Panickssery 2024)]]
- **3 · Self-Preference Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge** · Wataoka, Takahashi, Ri · [2410.21819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21819) · section 7 · **threat** · Judges favor low-perplexity text whether or not they wrote it. Self-preference is familiarity. → [[Judges favor familiar text, not their own (Wataoka 2024)]]
- **3 · Pride and Prejudice: LLM Amplifies Self-Bias in Self-Refinement** · Xu et al. · [2402.11436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11436) · ACL 2024 · section 6 · **threat** · Self-bias grows over self-refine rounds and with best-of-k pool size; outputs get more fluent, not better; bias follows the model's own style. → [[Self-refinement amplifies self-bias (Xu 2024)]]
- **2 · CREAM: Consistency Regularized Self-Rewarding LMs** · [2410.12735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12735) · section 6 · Self-rewarding gains fade after a few rounds in 7B models; authors blame bias that accumulates in the self-reward. Search summary only.
- **1 · Play Favorites: a statistical method for self-preference** · [2508.06709](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06709) · A judge scoring its own output higher is not always bias; the output may be better. Method caveat.
- **1 · Fairness or Fluency? Language bias of pairwise judges** · [2601.13649](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13649) · Perplexity explains only part of language bias in judges.
- Not rescored: Laurito et al. AI-AI bias ([2407.12856](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12856)), already covered in [[Models share one taste for text (Zatuchin 2026)]].

## Angle 3. Self-referential and self-modifying networks (sections 2 and 5)

## Angle 4. Monitoring signals that control learning (the controller gap)
- **3 · MAGELLAN: Metacognitive predictions of learning progress guide autotelic LLM agents** · Gaven et al. · [2502.07709](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07709) · ICML 2025 · section 5 · A readout of the agent's own last-layer state predicts competence; learning progress picks what to train on next. Closest case of a hidden-state signal that controls learning. → [[MAGELLAN - an agent predicts its own learning progress (Gaven 2025)]]
- **2 · Backpropamine: differentiable neuromodulated plasticity** · Miconi, Rawal, Clune, Stanley · [2002.10585](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10585) · ICLR 2019 · section 2 · The network outputs its own neuromodulator, which sets its plasticity rate. A concrete machine version of the Doya mapping. → [[Backpropamine - a network sets its own plasticity (Miconi 2019)]]
- **2 · Humans monitor learning progress in curiosity-driven exploration** · Ten, Kaushik, Oudeyer, Gottlieb · [Nature Communications 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-26196-w) · section 2 · Human task choice is best fit by models with a learning-progress term, not competence alone. Human basis for MAGELLAN. No note (cap); write next if section 2 needs a human control-of-learning source.
- **2 · Does This Look Familiar to You? Knowledge analysis via internal representations (KAMIR)** · [2509.07311](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07311) · section 5 · A classifier on hidden states finds data the model has not learned; training on less familiar data generalizes better. A hidden-state signal choosing training data, read by an outside classifier. Search summary only.
- **1 · SelectIT** · [2402.16705](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16705) · NeurIPS 2024 · The model's own output uncertainty selects instruction-tuning data. Output-level, not hidden state.
- **1 · Hidden Cartographer** · [2610.25215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25215) · Hidden states guide data annotation and selection [date and details unverified].
- Not searched in depth: human study-time allocation (Metcalfe and Kornell, region of proximal learning). Fleming and Daw 2017 is already in the repo.
